FEC Donor Watch · Daily Update

A new Trump-linked super PAC deploys nearly $48 million before its donor list is public

September 11, 2026. The material development since the September 10 report is the rapid emergence of No Going Back PAC Inc. (FEC C00960435), formed September 1. AdImpact data reported by The Washington Post show about $47.8 million in reservations across six Senate races and Pennsylvania's 10th District, while a September 10 FEC 48-hour notice provides the first detailed candidate-level spending trail. The committee's donor identities are not yet public.

Executive summary: No Going Back PAC moved from formation to a major multi-state advertising program in roughly one week. The Washington Post reported $47.8 million in reservations, about 89.5% of it concentrated in Michigan, Ohio, New Hampshire and Alaska Senate races. Reuters separately reported $24.9 million in TV bookings across six states as of September 11; the two figures differ in timing/scope and are not additive. A 48-hour FEC notice filed September 10 disclosed $2.47 million of September 8 independent expenditures, led by matching $935,988 entries supporting Ken Paxton and opposing James Talarico. Because the committee is new, its donor funding is not yet disclosed. Press reports connect it to Trump's broader political operation and MAGA Inc., but that is a network relationship, not evidence that MAGA Inc. legally controls the committee or that any particular donor funded a specific expenditure.

Disclosure timing
Funding source not yet public		 Reuters reports that No Going Back's donor disclosure will not arrive until later in October. The timing creates an information gap: voters can see independent expenditures and public ad reservations before they can see the committee's major funding sources. That is a disclosure-timing observation, not evidence of unlawful concealment.

Campaign and issue-interest sections

No Going Back PAC: broad election deployment

The strongest new signal is not a newly disclosed donor sector but a new spending vehicle. Public ad-booking data put the largest reservations in Michigan, Ohio, New Hampshire and Alaska Senate races. FEC 48-hour notices separately identify direct-mail support or opposition in Texas, North Carolina, Iowa and multiple House districts. Until donor receipts and creative/purpose records are more complete, attributing a narrower policy agenda would go beyond the public evidence.

Trump-linked network: relationship, not consolidation

Reuters reports that No Going Back shares infrastructure with MAGA Inc. but is not formally affiliated; The Washington Post describes it as connected to Trump's political operation. This report therefore treats No Going Back and MAGA Inc. as related contextual nodes, not as one treasury. Amounts are not combined as if they were the same committee.

Disclosure lag becomes analytically important

Independent-expenditure notices can reveal candidate-level activity quickly, while a newly formed monthly filer may not disclose its underlying receipts until its first applicable Form 3X report. The result is a temporary asymmetry: the public can see who is being supported or opposed before it can identify the largest funders.

Existing sector networks remain large

Fairshake, AIPAC/UDP and MAGA Inc. continue to have substantially larger disclosed cycle-scale financial bases than most single-race groups. The new No Going Back reservations matter because they show how quickly a separate vehicle can convert network resources or newly raised money into concentrated general-election advertising; the exact funding origin remains to be established by filings.

Observed electoral and policy outcomes

No election outcome yet for the new spending. The No Going Back reservations and September 8 independent expenditures are general-election activity. The 2026 federal general election is still ahead, so this report does not calculate a “success rate” or treat ad reservations as evidence of electoral effectiveness.

No policy outcome can yet be attributed to the new committee. The candidates above have pre-existing legislative or executive records. The temporal sequence therefore runs mostly from policy record to outside spending, not from spending to a subsequent policy action. Even where spending precedes a future vote, additional evidence would be required to establish influence.

Methodology and limitations

  • Materiality: this update prioritizes newly reported multi-million-dollar reservations, new 24/48-hour FEC notices, donor concentrations and policy developments that materially change the prior report. Smaller baseline movements are omitted unless they change interpretation.
  • Reservations are not expenditures: AdImpact advertising reservations are forward bookings and can be revised. They are shown separately from FEC independent expenditures and are never added to filed Schedule E/F24 amounts as if they were the same transaction.
  • Fast FEC notices vs. slower receipt disclosure: 24/48-hour independent-expenditure notices can appear before a committee's next regular Form 3X. For monthly filers, the FEC's September report closes Aug. 31 and is due Sept. 20; the October report closes Sept. 30 and is due Oct. 20. A committee formed Sept. 1 therefore can have visible September independent expenditures before its first regular donor report covering September activity.
  • Committee separation: No Going Back PAC, MAGA Inc., America PAC, candidate committees and party committees are treated as separate legal entities. Press-reported relationships are labeled as such and are not used to combine cash or receipts.
  • Legislation selection: candidate legislation is included to show verifiable public policy records. Where the spender's issue rationale is unknown, the report says so rather than inventing a policy motive. Non-legislators are explicitly identified as having no current legislative voting record.
  • Causation: campaign contributions, independent expenditures, access, legislative alignment and subsequent governmental action can coexist without one causing another. This report flags only documented relationships and does not imply quid pro quo without evidence.
  • Source hierarchy: FEC filings and official legislative sources are preferred for transactions and bills; Reuters, The Washington Post and similar reporting are used for current ad reservations and organizational context not yet reflected in regular FEC summaries.
