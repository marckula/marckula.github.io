America PAC opens a four-state issue-ad front as crypto lobbying moves into senators’ home states

September 9, 2026. Two developments are material since the September 8 report: America PAC launched online and streaming ads against four Democratic Senate candidates around transgender-policy issues, and crypto and banking groups intensified direct state-level lobbying ahead of the September 15 Senate procedural vote on digital-asset market structure.

Executive summary: America PAC's new ads target Sherrod Brown in Ohio, Josh Turek in Iowa, James Talarico in Texas and Mary Peltola in Alaska. A tracker built from FEC Schedule E filings currently shows cumulative America PAC opposition spending of $648,371, $247,860, $1,487,646 and $146,218, respectively; those figures cover all reported opposition activity for each candidate and should not be read as the cost of the new ad batch alone. Separately, Reuters reports that Coinbase-backed Stand With Crypto generated nearly 50,000 calls and emails to Congress in August and arranged in-person lawmaker contacts as the crypto and banking industries contest the CLARITY Act. These are documented political-spending and advocacy channels; they are not evidence of coordination, quid pro quo or a policy reward.

Crypto legislation and direct advocacy

H.R. 3633 — Digital Asset Market Clarity Act

The Senate Banking Committee advanced H.R. 3633 by 15–9 on May 14, 2026. The bill is scheduled for a Senate procedural vote on September 15. The crypto industry supports a federal digital-asset market-structure framework; banking groups have raised concerns including stablecoin-related competition for deposits and lending capacity.

The Sept. 9 development is not a new FEC receipt but a broader political-channel signal: election money is operating alongside nonprofit advertising, grassroots contacts and organized lobbying.

Stand With Crypto → Sen. Raphael Warnock office

Reuters reports that Stand With Crypto's Georgia chapter met staff at Sen. Raphael Warnock's field office and was arranging additional constituent outreach. Reuters also reports that Warnock had voted against advancing the CLARITY Act in the Senate Banking Committee.

Assessment: this establishes organized constituent contact with a lawmaker's staff and a known legislative position. It does not establish that access changed the senator's vote, that any benefit was offered, or that campaign spending caused a legislative action.

Observed outcomes — descriptive, not causal

America PAC's four newly highlighted Senate races: no general-election outcome exists yet, so the new advertising can only be recorded as spending and messaging, not as electoral effectiveness.

CLARITY Act: the Senate Banking Committee's 15–9 May vote is an observed legislative outcome that predates this week's home-state lobbying surge. The next scheduled procedural vote is Sept. 15; no inference is made about its result.

TX-09 AI spending: Mealer's May 26 runoff win and Cain's loss remain observed outcomes following outside spending. Candidate quality, endorsements, district dynamics, counter-spending and other factors prevent causal attribution to the PAC expenditures.

Methodology and limitations

  • This edition compares newly reported activity with the September 8 page. A development is treated as material when it adds a significant spending deployment, a new donor/network pathway, a direct legislative-advocacy channel, or a consequential observed outcome.
  • FEC regular committee summaries can lag newly filed activity by up to 48 hours. The FEC's regular independent-expenditure summaries do not necessarily include the newest 24- and 48-hour reports.
  • The America PAC candidate amounts cited for the new targets are cumulative figures from a tracker that states it derives them from FEC Schedule E filings and excludes a small number of implausible anomalous filings. They are used to show scale, not to assign the full amount to the Sept. 9 ad batch. The official FEC committee page remains the primary source for committee status and regular-summary finances.
  • Independent expenditures are outside spending made to support or oppose candidates; they are not contributions directly to candidate committees.
  • A 501(c)(4) such as Cedar Innovation Foundation is not equivalent to a federally registered super PAC. Its undisclosed donor funding is not combined with Fairshake's FEC-disclosed donor ledger.
  • Candidate legislation is selected for relevance to issue areas used by outside-spending groups. Selection does not imply that a vote or bill motivated the spending.
  • For candidates no longer serving in a legislature, prior legislative records are clearly labeled historical; for candidates without legislative service, only attributable policy statements or executive actions are used.
  • Support/opposition, access, policy alignment, election results and later governmental actions are recorded descriptively and are not treated as proof of coordination, causation, preferential treatment or quid pro quo.
  • No candidate, party, committee, donor, legislation or policy is scored or ranked.
