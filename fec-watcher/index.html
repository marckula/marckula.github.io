FEC Donor Watch · September 14, 2026

Crypto policy reaches a new ethics compromise as another concentrated PAC network comes into view

Two developments clear the publication threshold today: Senate negotiators released a revised CLARITY Act with stronger conflict-of-interest and enforcement provisions in an issue area backed by unusually large crypto-sector political spending; and a review of federal and Tennessee disclosures adds a detailed Blackburn–Club for Growth funding network to the donor map. The latter transactions mostly occurred earlier in 2026, so they are presented as newly surfaced network context rather than as September 14 transfers.

Executive summary: Fairshake, whose stated mission is to elect candidates favoring clearer blockchain regulation, reports $137.41 million in receipts and $112.99 million cash on hand through July 31. On September 14, Senate Republicans released a revised version of the CLARITY Act after negotiations added ethics provisions; AP reports the compromise would add state-attorney-general enforcement and require divestment or blind trusts for certain significant crypto interests. Separately, FEC records show Marsha for Senate contributed $4.8 million to Club for Growth Action on May 8. Club for Growth Action reported $33.05 million in receipts through July 31; its two largest disclosed funding sources visible in the reviewed filing data were Jeff Yass, $15 million, and Marsha for Senate, $4.8 million, together equal to about 59.9% of total receipts. Tennessee disclosures reported by Tennessee Lookout show the related School Freedom Fund transferred $4.81 million to Tennessee Freedom Fund, which bought ads supporting Blackburn and opposing John Rose. These are distinct committees and accounts; similar dollar amounts do not establish that the same dollars were passed through or that any contribution caused a vote or policy outcome.

Campaign and issue-interest developments

Crypto: political spending and policy movement remain closely timed, but causation is unproven

Fairshake says its purpose is to support candidates who favor regulatory and legal clarity for blockchain networks. The Senate Banking Committee advanced H.R. 3633, the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, 15–9 on May 14. Reuters reported on Sept. 14 that Senate Republicans released revised text with new ethics language; AP reports the negotiated additions include state-attorney-general enforcement and divestment or blind-trust requirements for certain significant crypto holdings. These facts establish temporal and issue alignment, not that Fairshake contributions caused the revisions or the bill's advancement.

Blackburn / Club for Growth: concentrated federal funding intersects a state race

FEC data show the Blackburn Tennessee Victory Fund raised $9.02M through June 30 and reported $2.30M in transfers to affiliated committees. Marsha for Senate separately reported $2.10M in transfers received from other authorized committees, then contributed $4.8M to Club for Growth Action on May 8. Club for Growth Action's filing shows that $4.8M alongside $15M from Jeff Yass. The records document a concentrated funding structure; they do not by themselves trace a particular donor's dollars to a particular Tennessee ad.

Education / school-choice alignment is real, but not unique to the supported candidate

School Freedom Fund states that it supports candidates favoring school choice, and Club for Growth's Blackburn endorsement cited her school-freedom position. Blackburn voted against an amendment that would have eliminated the federal scholarship program from H.R. 1 and later voted for the Senate substitute. But Rose also voted Aye on final House concurrence in the Senate-amended H.R. 1. That comparison matters: the network's support/oppose split cannot be reduced to a single federal school-choice vote.

Disclosure boundaries

Federal candidate committees, federal super PACs and Tennessee state PACs file under different systems and schedules. Similar-sized transactions across related organizations are analytically notable but should not be added or equated without transaction-level evidence. This report therefore describes the sequence and common organizational ties while avoiding a same-dollar pass-through claim.

Observed outcome analysis

Electoral: Blackburn defeated Rose in Tennessee's Aug. 6 Republican gubernatorial primary. The Club for Growth-aligned spending preceded that result, but the available evidence does not isolate the effect of those expenditures from candidate spending, endorsements, other PAC activity, voter preferences or other campaign factors.

Policy — education: The federal scholarship tax credit enacted through H.R. 1 is now moving into implementation; the IRS lists Tennessee among states electing to participate for 2027. Blackburn's votes are consistent with the school-choice agenda highlighted by Club for Growth, but Rose also voted for final House concurrence, which limits how much can be inferred from that single legislative outcome.

Policy — crypto: The CLARITY Act advanced from Senate Banking in May and received revised ethics language on Sept. 14 ahead of further Senate action. This is an observable policy development in an area where Fairshake and the broader crypto industry have deployed substantial political resources. The inclusion of stronger ethics and state-enforcement provisions also shows that the final policy trajectory is shaped by multiple competing actors, not simply donor preferences.

Methodology and limitations

  • Publication threshold: The report updates today because the CLARITY Act received a material new ethics/enforcement compromise on Sept. 14 and because the Blackburn/Club for Growth records add a concentrated, multi-committee donor network not present in the prior report. Most Blackburn-network transactions themselves occurred earlier in 2026; the novelty is the added network analysis, not a claim that those transfers happened today.
  • Primary records first: FEC committee summaries establish federal receipts, disbursements and cash. Raw electronic filing data are used for the $4.8M Marsha for Senate contribution and $15M Yass contributions. Official Senate and House roll calls establish candidate votes; IRS records establish §25F implementation.
  • State/federal separation: Marsha for Senate, Club for Growth Action, School Freedom Fund and Tennessee Freedom Fund are separate entities/accounts. Similar transaction amounts and shared officers/addresses are relevant context but are not treated as proof that specific dollars were transferred through.
  • Regular-report lag: FEC summary independent-expenditure totals exclude 24- and 48-hour notices and may lag newly filed information. Media reservations and state reports also follow different reporting schedules.
  • Concentration calculation: $15M from Yass plus $4.8M from Marsha for Senate equals $19.8M, or about 59.9% of Club for Growth Action's $33.054M receipts through July 31. This is a treasury-level concentration metric, not race-specific attribution.
  • Causation: Contributions, expenditures, policy positions, access and outcomes are presented chronologically. Correlation, shared issue positions or subsequent policy movement are not treated as evidence of a bargain or quid pro quo.
