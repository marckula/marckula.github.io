FEC Donor Watch · September 12, 2026

Texas draws a new $32 million Senate-leadership ad tranche as No Going Back reservations exceed $74 million

The material changes since the September 11 report are a new Senate Leadership Fund-linked Texas vehicle entering the Paxton–Talarico race, a further expansion of No Going Back PAC's national reservations, and a fresh Texas Organizing Project federal independent-expenditure notice. Ad reservations, FEC independent expenditures and direct campaign spending are different categories and are not added together unless the underlying records are comparable.

Executive summary: Texas PAC, a new vehicle connected to Senate Leadership Fund (SLF PAC), purchased an initial $32 million broadcast-TV tranche supporting Ken Paxton on September 11; Punchbowl reports the vehicle expects to spend more than $50 million in Texas. SLF itself reported $253.66 million in receipts and $238.61 million cash on hand through June 30. Separately, Reuters reports No Going Back PAC's reservations have grown from the roughly $47.8 million documented in yesterday's report to more than $74 million across 15 House races and six Senate states—an increase of at least $26.2 million, or about 54.8%. A September 11 filing also shows Texas Organizing Project PAC spending $414,042 to oppose Paxton and support James Talarico and Christian Menefee. No credible evidence reviewed connects these fresh expenditures to an appointment, contract, regulatory favor or other reciprocal governmental benefit.

Campaign and issue-interest analysis

Texas Senate: multiple independent networks converge

Texas PAC/SLF entered with an initial ~$32M broadcast reservation supporting Paxton, on top of the previously reported MAGA Inc. $10M Texas buy split between $5M supporting Paxton and $5M opposing Talarico. America PAC had disclosed $2.388M through Sept. 9, while Texas Organizing Project PAC filed $207,500 opposing Paxton and $168,650 supporting Talarico on Sept. 11. These are separate committees and expenditures; their treasuries are not combined.

Texas issue messaging: taxes plus candidate contrast

MAGA Inc.'s earlier Texas advertising used Talarico's property-tax voting record as a message. The official Texas House Journal confirms that Talarico voted nay on SB 2 on May 1, 2019; the bill was later signed by the governor on June 12, 2019. Paxton, now attorney general rather than a legislator, has used his office to enforce a newer state property-tax law, SB 1851, including an Aug. 17, 2026 letter campaign to more than 110 cities. Both records predate the current September outside spending and therefore are policy context, not subsequent rewards.

Michigan Senate: No Going Back adds a $15.7M reservation

Reuters reports $15.7M of No Going Back's reservations in Michigan, supporting Mike Rogers and opposing Abdul El-Sayed. One ad reuses prior El-Sayed policing comments. Neither candidate currently holds a legislative office, so there is no current legislative voting record to map to the new expenditure. Current campaign platforms are treated as stated positions, not enacted policy.

Reservation data require caution

AdImpact reservations can be changed or canceled, and FEC Schedule E notices reflect reportable independent expenditures rather than all future reservations. The same buy can appear at different dollar values in press reports depending on when the tracker was queried. This report therefore keeps “reserved,” “reported to the FEC,” and “spent by a candidate” separate.

Methodology and limitations

  • Source hierarchy: FEC committee summaries and 24/48-hour notices first; official legislative records for bills/votes; then Reuters, The Texas Tribune, Punchbowl and other reporting for ad reservations and organizational relationships. Candidate sites are used only for the candidate's own stated policies.
  • Reservations are not expenditures: future ad reservations can change. They are kept separate from FEC-reported independent expenditures and from candidate spending.
  • FEC timing: regular committee summaries can lag real-time 24/48-hour independent-expenditure notices. The FEC itself notes that its regular IE totals do not include those notices.
  • Donor attribution: a contribution to a parent or aligned super PAC does not prove that the donor funded a particular race, advertisement or affiliate. Network relationships are described without consolidating legally separate treasuries.
  • “Other Receipts”: this is an FEC reporting category, not a synonym for anonymous money. Transaction-level filings are needed to identify sources.
  • Candidate policy scope: detailed candidate records are added when fresh candidate-specific FEC activity is at least roughly $150,000 or when a new/expanded named-race ad reservation is at least roughly $10 million, plus the principal opponent for comparison. This is a reporting-scope rule, not an evaluation of a candidate.
  • Media-rate caveat: candidate campaigns generally receive lower broadcast/radio unit rates than outside groups, so raw ad dollars are not equivalent measures of audience reach.
  • Causation: donor support, policy alignment, access and subsequent official action are reported separately. Temporal sequence and correlation are not treated as proof of influence or quid pro quo.
