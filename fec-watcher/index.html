Crypto political network shifts from candidate spending toward a seven-figure CLARITY Act push

September 8, 2026. The material change since the prior report is a new national issue-ad campaign by the Cedar Innovation Foundation, a 501(c)(4) reported as linked to the Fairshake political network, supporting federal digital-asset market-structure legislation. This edition also adds the requested legislation and policy records for materially supported and opposed candidates.

Executive summary: Punchbowl News reported September 8 that Cedar Innovation Foundation is launching a seven-figure cable buy across three national ads supporting the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, ahead of an initial Senate vote scheduled for September 15. This is a meaningful change in channel: a network best known for super-PAC candidate spending is now also associated in public reporting with direct issue advocacy around a named bill. Cedar does not disclose its donors, so this report does not attribute Cedar's ad money to Coinbase, Ripple, Andreessen Horowitz, or any other Fairshake donor. Fairshake itself remains a separately disclosed FEC committee with $112.99 million cash on hand through July 31.

Federal crypto legislation and political alignment

H.R. 3633 — Digital Asset Market Clarity Act of 2025

Sponsored by Rep. French Hill and introduced May 29, 2025, H.R. 3633 creates a federal digital-asset market-structure framework, including defined roles for the Commodity Futures Trading Commission and Securities and Exchange Commission. The House passed it 294–134 on July 17, 2025. The current Cedar campaign explicitly supports this bill; Punchbowl reports an initial Senate vote is set for Sept. 15.

Separately, Reuters reported August 24 that Coinbase-backed Stand With Crypto endorsed 32 incumbent House candidates and that all 32 had voted for the CLARITY Act. That is an observable policy screen used by an advocacy group. It does not establish that contributions or endorsements caused any lawmaker's vote.

Observed outcomes

Mealer–Cain: Defending Our Values supported Mealer and opposed Cain; Mealer won the May 26 TX-09 Republican runoff. The Texas Tribune reported that several other outside groups also spent heavily in the race. The result therefore cannot be attributed to the AI PAC alone.

Texas Senate: the recent MAGA Inc. and America PAC expenditures involve a general-election contest that has not yet produced an election outcome.

CLARITY Act: the House vote is an observed legislative outcome from July 2025. The Cedar ad campaign is new advocacy around the pending Senate stage; the Sept. 15 vote has not yet occurred.

Campaign and issue interests

Crypto: candidate election activity plus direct market-structure legislation advocacy. The Fairshake committees disclose donors to the FEC; Cedar's 501(c)(4) donor source is not publicly disclosed.

AI: Defending Our Values backs candidates associated with stronger AI guardrails, security and China-competition themes. Its supported and opposed Texas candidates show both legislative and non-legislative policy records.

Texas Senate: MAGA Inc. and America PAC have concentrated recent outside spending on supporting Paxton and opposing Talarico. The two committees are separate; parallel activity does not establish coordination.

Methodology and limitations

  • This update compares newly available disclosures and reporting with the September 7 report. A development is treated as material when it introduces a high-dollar deployment, a new donor/network pathway, a direct connection to pending legislation, or a significant observed outcome.
  • FEC regular committee summaries can lag newly filed activity and exclude the newest 24- and 48-hour independent-expenditure reports. The FEC specifically notes that newly filed summary data can take up to 48 hours to appear.
  • A 501(c)(4) such as Cedar Innovation Foundation is not equivalent to a federally registered super PAC. Cedar's donor funding is not publicly disclosed, so this report does not merge Cedar's funding with Fairshake's FEC-disclosed donor ledger.
  • Candidate legislation is selected for relevance to the issue areas associated with the outside-spending network; selection does not imply that the legislation motivated the spending.
  • For non-legislator candidates, the report uses only attributable policy statements or executive actions and explicitly notes the absence of a legislative voting record.
  • Independent expenditures are outside spending, not direct transfers to candidate committees. Support/opposition, policy alignment, election results and later governmental actions are recorded descriptively and are not treated as proof of coordination, causation or quid pro quo.
  • No candidate, party, committee, donor, legislation or policy is scored or ranked.
