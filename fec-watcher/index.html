FEC Donor Watch · September 17, 2026

Trump-aligned groups top $130 million in ad reservations as Michigan outside spending escalates on both sides

The clearest new trend is a rapid expansion of late-cycle outside spending through newly formed committees whose advertising is visible before their donor histories are fully processed. Associated Press reports that Trump-aligned groups have now reserved more than $130 million in advertising: more than $98.5 million through No Going Back PAC, about $27 million through Safety and Affordability PAC, and about $11 million through MAGA Inc. itself. In Michigan, Senate Majority PAC separately added $10 million for Abdul El-Sayed, bringing its state commitment to $30 million, while Republican-aligned groups have announced substantially larger commitments for Mike Rogers. The new evidence sharpens two recurring themes: concentration of financing in a small number of large committees and donors, and disclosure lag when new political vehicles are activated shortly before an election.

Executive summary: No Going Back's reported reservations have risen from more than $74M in the prior report to more than $98.5M today — an increase of at least $24.5M, or about 33%. AP reports that No Going Back and Safety and Affordability were both organized on September 1 and share the same treasurer as MAGA Inc.; that personnel connection and AP's reporting support describing a political network, but do not establish that any particular MAGA Inc. donor financed a particular new ad. MAGA Inc.'s own FEC summary shows $400.68M in receipts and $403.45M cash on hand through July 31. On the Democratic side, SMP's FEC summary shows $174.22M in receipts and $126.48M cash through June 30; Washington Post analysis identifies affiliated nonprofit Majority Forward as a $61M source of SMP funding, about 35% of SMP's reported receipts, while Majority Forward's underlying donors are not publicly disclosed. No credible evidence reviewed for this update establishes a quid pro quo, public contract, appointment, regulatory favor or other official reward tied to the new spending.

Campaign and issue-interest developments

New vehicles, old network, delayed donor visibility

AP reports that No Going Back and Safety and Affordability were both organized on Sept. 1 and list the same treasurer as MAGA Inc. That is credible evidence of shared political infrastructure. It is not, by itself, evidence that MAGA Inc.'s known donors funded a specific No Going Back or Safety and Affordability advertisement. The FEC notes that newly filed summary data can take time to appear, and 24/48-hour independent-expenditure notices are separate from regular-report summary totals.

MAGA Inc. donor concentration remains relevant baseline context

MAGA Inc.'s FEC page shows $400.68M in cycle receipts through July 31. Reuters' review of a prior filing found that three sources — Greg Brockman, Foris DAX/Crypto.com and Konstantin Sokolov — supplied $56M of roughly $102M raised between July 1 and Dec. 22, 2025. That roughly 55% concentration applies to that fundraising window, not automatically to the newer PACs or to today's $130M reservation pool.

Michigan becomes a two-sided outside-spending concentration point

Axios reports SMP's new $10M tranche takes its support for Abdul El-Sayed to $30M. It also reports Republican-side commitments including $51M from Senate Leadership Fund, roughly $18M from a MAGA-aligned super PAC, and about $10M already spent or reserved by Great Lakes Conservatives Fund. The totals are commitments/reservations from different sources and should not be treated as candidate receipts.

Late reservations buy less airtime per dollar

AP notes that candidate campaigns receive preferential broadcast rates in the final 60 days, while super PACs do not. That means a late $10M or $20M outside-group reservation may buy materially less television inventory than the same nominal amount booked earlier. Dollar totals therefore measure financial deployment, not equal units of voter exposure.

Observed electoral and policy outcomes

Michigan: El-Sayed won the Democratic primary on Aug. 4; the new $10M SMP increment was announced afterward. It is therefore better understood as a post-primary resource allocation than as a cause of that primary outcome. The general election has not occurred, so no electoral effectiveness conclusion is possible from the current commitments.

NY-17: The election outcome is not yet observed. AP's finding that the new Trump-aligned groups are the district's largest outside spenders establishes financial concentration, not electoral effect.

Crypto carry-forward: On Sept. 15, the Senate rejected cloture on H.R. 3633, 49–50. That remains a concrete policy outcome associated with a policy area that has attracted very large campaign spending, but it cuts against simplistic claims that contributions or independent expenditures necessarily produce desired legislation.

Influence, access and potential reward evidence

Documented political access: Axios reports that El-Sayed met privately with Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer and other Democratic senators at the DSCC as Democratic groups worked to unify around his candidacy, while the Schumer-aligned SMP increased its Michigan commitment. This is credible evidence of party-political access and alignment. It is not evidence that the SMP spending was exchanged for a vote, endorsement, appointment or governmental action; Axios also reports that El-Sayed had not committed to backing Schumer for leader.

Trump-aligned network responsiveness: AP reports the new spending wave followed requests from vulnerable Republican campaigns and allies for additional help. That supports a political-strategy interpretation of the spending. It does not establish an official governmental reward.

No official reward identified: This review found no credible new evidence tying the Sept. 16–17 spending developments to a public contract, appointment, regulatory outcome, legislative favor or other reciprocal governmental benefit. Network ties, shared treasurers, meetings and policy alignment are reported as such and are not treated as proof of quid pro quo.

Methodology and limitations

  • Update threshold: This page is replaced only when new information materially changes the scale, structure, donor concentration, candidate targeting, policy outcome or credible influence/access record. Today's >$130M network total and the new $10M Michigan tranche met that threshold.
  • Reservations are not final expenditures: Ad reservations can be changed or cancelled. They are kept separate from FEC-reported disbursements and candidate receipts.
  • Independent expenditures are not candidate contributions: Super-PAC spending supporting or opposing a candidate is not money received or controlled by that candidate.
  • Disclosure timing: New committees may place ads before periodic reports disclose their donors. FEC regular-report summaries also do not necessarily include the newest 24/48-hour independent-expenditure notices.
  • Affiliation is not dollar tracing: A shared treasurer, vendor, analytics code or reported network affiliation does not establish which donor funded a specific communication. Donor attribution is made only where filings or credible reporting establish it.
  • Issue inference: Committee names are not treated as proof of issue motive. Legislation is selected for relevance to documented campaign themes or known donor-network interests, and alignment is never treated as evidence that money caused a vote.
  • Non-legislators: Candidates not currently serving in a legislature are explicitly identified as having no current legislative voting record. Campaign proposals and prior executive actions are labeled separately.
