FEC Donor Watch · September 23, 2026

SLF-linked Texas spending commitment reaches $100 million while the new Texas PAC still has no processed donor ledger

The material new development is the Senate Leadership Fund network's escalation in the Texas U.S. Senate race. The Texas Tribune reported September 22 that the SLF-linked Texas PAC will add $30 million in advertising, bringing the network's planned Texas buy to $100 million supporting Ken Paxton and opposing James Talarico. The newest publicly indexed 48-hour expenditure notice reviewed here shows Texas PAC at $61.26 million calendar-year-to-date in independent expenditures as of September 15. The difference matters: the $100 million figure is a media commitment/reservation, while FEC expenditure notices document spending as it is distributed or obligated under campaign-finance reporting rules.

Executive summary: Texas PAC registered with the FEC on August 28, 2026 as an independent-expenditure-only committee. Its processed FEC profile still shows no receipt summary or donor transactions, even as press reporting puts its planned Texas advertising at $100 million. The broader SLF PAC reported $253.66 million in receipts and $238.61 million cash on hand through June 30. One Nation, a 501(c)(4) whose underlying donors are not publicly disclosed, had supplied at least $46 million to SLF by that date—about 18.1% of SLF's receipts. Named SLF donors also include Miriam Adelson, Paul Singer, Elon Musk and Ken Griffin, but donations to SLF cannot automatically be attributed to Texas PAC or to support for Paxton; reporting on Griffin, for example, specifically said he did not plan to support Paxton. No credible evidence reviewed for this update establishes a quid pro quo, appointment, contract, regulatory exemption or other reciprocal governmental reward tied to the Texas spending.

Campaign and issue-interest developments

Reservation totals and FEC expenditures are different measures

The newest publicly indexed Texas PAC 48-hour notice reviewed here was filed September 17 for September 15 printing/postage. It reports matching $373,961.08 support-Paxton and $373,961.07 oppose-Talarico entries and a $61.26M calendar-year-to-date amount. Texas Tribune's $100M figure is the network's reported advertising commitment after adding another $30M in bookings. The two measures should not be added together.

Donor disclosure is lagging spending disclosure

Texas PAC registered August 28 as a quarterly super PAC. Its processed FEC profile currently says no 2025–2026 financial summary is available and shows no donor transactions. The FEC notes this can occur while data are still processing, because no activity of that type has been filed, or because the relevant filing deadline has not passed. The absence of processed donor data is therefore a disclosure-timing fact, not evidence of an undisclosed illegal donor.

Direct-campaign fundraising remains very different from outside spending

Through June 30, Talarico's principal campaign committee reported $68.56M receipts and $21.55M cash on hand. Paxton's authorized committees reported $9.25M receipts and $1.76M cash. The Texas PAC/SLF expenditures are legally separate outside spending and cannot be added to Paxton's candidate receipts.

Foreign-land policy provides a concrete record for comparison

Separate from SLF's broad electoral purpose, the Texas campaign has featured disputes over Chinese investment and land ownership. That makes Texas SB 17 a verifiable policy record to compare: Talarico voted against final House passage in 2025, while Paxton, as attorney general, has since proposed rules to administer the enacted law. The records show a policy difference; they do not establish that donor money caused either position.

Observed electoral and policy outcomes

No Texas electoral outcome yet: the $100M Texas commitment establishes spending scale, not effectiveness. No causal claim about voter behavior is warranted before or after the election without additional evidence.

Policy outcome already occurred before the spending surge: Texas SB 17 became law effective September 1, 2025. Talarico's 2025 Nay vote and Paxton's March 2026 rulemaking both predate Texas PAC's August 28, 2026 registration. The chronology rules out treating those actions as rewards for Texas PAC's later expenditures.

Funding structure can matter without proving influence: the combination of a newly registered spending vehicle, tens of millions of dollars in rapid independent expenditures, and delayed receipt disclosure means voters can see the electoral intervention before they can see Texas PAC's complete funding sources. That is a transparency/timing observation, not evidence of unlawful coordination.

Influence, access and potential reward evidence

No candidate-level donor attribution from SLF alone: large donors to SLF should not automatically be described as Paxton donors. The strongest counterexample in the reviewed record is Ken Griffin: Washington Post identifies a $10M SLF contribution, while Wall Street Journal reporting said Griffin had no plans to help Paxton. This weakens any attempt to infer each SLF donor's intent from Texas PAC's candidate targeting.

Older donor-policy tension retained as context: Hearst Newspapers reported September 21 that Paxton and affiliated political committees had received about $40,000 from donors connected to firms facilitating Chinese real-estate investment in Texas. Separately, Paxton's office is implementing SB 17's restrictions on certain foreign land acquisitions. The amounts are small relative to the race's major funding flows, and no credible evidence reviewed shows that those donors received an exemption, contract, appointment, access arrangement or favorable regulatory outcome. The documented policy action is restrictive rather than an obvious reward.

No new quid-pro-quo evidence: this review found no credible public evidence tying the newly reported $100M advertising commitment to an appointment, government contract, regulatory favor, legislative concession or reciprocal policy benefit.

Methodology and limitations

  • Update threshold: the Sept. 22 announcement increasing the SLF-linked Texas advertising commitment to $100M materially changes the scale of outside spending and therefore met the publication threshold.
  • Bookings are not the same as expenditures: the $100M figure comes from reported media bookings/commitments. The $61.26M figure comes from a 48-hour FEC notice's calendar-year-to-date field as of Sept. 15. They are not summed.
  • Texas PAC donor data are incomplete: the FEC processed profile currently displays no financial summary or donor rows. The report does not infer unreported donors from SLF's donor list.
  • Network attribution: Texas Tribune describes Texas PAC as an SLF-associated/connected vehicle. FEC registration establishes Texas PAC as its own independent-expenditure-only committee. Donor transfers between entities should be stated only when supported by actual receipt records.
  • Concentration calculation: One Nation's minimum $46M through June 30 divided by SLF's official $253.66M receipts equals about 18.1%. The Washington Post's later $70.74M figure is shown separately because its measurement date is not identical to the FEC June-30 summary.
  • Independent expenditures: FEC data explicitly state these expenditures are not funds directly given to or spent by the candidate. No coordination is inferred.
  • 48-hour data: current-cycle independent-expenditure files can contain original and amended notices; figures should be de-duplicated before aggregation. This report uses the filing's stated calendar-year-to-date amount rather than mechanically summing notices.
  • Policy-record selection: SB 17 is prioritized because foreign land/investment is a documented issue in the current Texas campaign and both candidates have verifiable official records on the same law.
  • No causal inference: donor activity, ad spending, candidate votes, agency actions and election outcomes are not treated as proof that one caused another without credible evidence.
