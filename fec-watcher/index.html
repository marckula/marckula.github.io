FEC Donor Watch · September 13, 2026

Texas PAC’s initial Paxton broadcast tranche is revised to $51.3 million

The meaningful change since the September 12 report is a large upward revision to the Senate Leadership Fund-linked Texas PAC buy, plus the first airing of its support-and-opposition ads. The updated AdImpact-based figure is a media reservation estimate, not yet a substitute for transaction-level FEC independent-expenditure reporting.

Executive summary: The Texas Tribune updated its reporting on September 12 to show Texas PAC purchased an initial $51.3 million broadcast-TV tranche in Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio and Austin—up $19.3 million, or about 60.3%, from the roughly $32 million figure carried in the prior report. Two Texas PAC ads began airing September 12: one supporting Ken Paxton and one opposing James Talarico. AdImpact’s broader Texas tally now puts Republican groups at $81.1 million in aired/placed ads plus future reservations through the end of September, versus $43.7 million for Democrats, nearly all from Talarico’s campaign. These dollar categories are not perfectly comparable because candidate campaigns receive lower broadcast rates than super PACs. No new public evidence reviewed ties the new spending to an appointment, contract, regulatory favor or other reciprocal governmental benefit.

Campaign and issue-interest developments

Texas: outside funding rapidly concentrates

Texas PAC’s updated $51.3M initial tranche accounts for roughly 63% of the $81.1M Republican-group Texas ad/reservation total reported by AdImpact through the end of September. MAGA Inc. had already booked $10M in Texas, and America PAC has reported more than $2.6M of calls, texts and printed advertising. The principal change today is not the arrival of another network, but the substantially larger scale of the SLF-linked vehicle.

Issue messaging becomes observable

The two Texas PAC ads that began airing Sept. 12 clarify the campaign themes attached to the spending. The positive spot highlights a sex-trafficking prosecution by Paxton as attorney general. The negative spot uses Talarico’s comments defending transgender children and reprises a “for they/them, not you” formulation. The existence of this messaging establishes what the ads emphasize; it does not establish why any particular donor contributed to SLF or Texas PAC.

Source opacity remains material

SLF’s FEC summary shows a very large disclosed treasury, but Issue One identifies $70.74M—about 28% of receipts through June—as coming from affiliated nonprofit One Nation, whose original donors are not publicly disclosed. Separately disclosed SLF donors such as Adelson, Singer and Griffin cannot automatically be treated as the funders of the Texas PAC tranche.

Reservation totals are not FEC expenditure totals

AdImpact estimates track media placements and reservations. FEC independent-expenditure filings report legally defined expenditures and can arrive on a different timetable. The FEC also notes that the independent-expenditure totals shown in a committee’s regular summary exclude 24- and 48-hour reports. This report therefore labels each dollar figure by source and category rather than adding unlike totals.

Observed outcomes

Electoral: No general-election outcome exists yet for the Texas or Michigan Senate races. This report does not infer effectiveness from ad volume or spending balance.

Campaign-market outcome: The measurable near-term change is financial and operational: Texas PAC’s initial tranche was revised upward from ~$32M to $51.3M and two ads began airing Sept. 12. AdImpact’s broader Texas tally now shows $81.1M in Republican-group activity/reservations and $43.7M in Democratic activity through Sept. 30. Because candidate campaigns obtain discounted broadcast rates, the nominal dollar gap does not translate directly into an equivalent gap in purchased audience.

Policy: Talarico’s 2023 SB 15 committee-calendar vote and the law’s Sept. 1, 2023 effective date long predate the 2026 outside spending. The sequence is consistent with outside groups selecting an existing policy difference for campaign messaging, not with a contribution causing the legislative action.

Methodology and limitations

  • Change threshold: This update publishes because the Texas PAC estimate moved from roughly $32M to $51.3M—an approximately 60% revision—and the ads began airing. Smaller routine filing changes were not promoted to headline developments.
  • Primary records first: Committee financial baselines use FEC data; legislative records use official Texas Legislature / Texas House sources. Reporting from the Texas Tribune is used for AdImpact reservation data and the connection between Texas PAC and SLF.
  • Different money categories stay separate: Media reservations, completed ad spending, FEC independent expenditures, direct candidate spending, contributions to super PACs and nonprofit transfers are not interchangeable.
  • FEC timing: FEC committee summary pages can lag new filings, and their regular-report independent-expenditure totals exclude 24- and 48-hour notices. Fresh media reservations may therefore precede a matching transaction-level FEC total.
  • Donor attribution: A donor to SLF is not labeled as the source of Texas PAC’s Paxton advertising absent evidence tracing that donor’s funds to the Texas vehicle or expenditure. One Nation’s original donors are not disclosed publicly.
  • Causation: Contributions, policy positions, access and subsequent official actions are presented chronologically and descriptively. Correlation or policy alignment is not treated as evidence of a bargain, coordination or quid pro quo.
