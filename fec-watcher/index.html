FEC Donor Watch · Daily Update

Outside spending concentrated further in Texas while an AI-policy PAC filed a new burst

September 7, 2026. The material changes since the prior report are new September 6 independent-expenditure filings from Elon Musk's America PAC in the Texas Senate race and a separate burst of filings from Defending Our Values PAC, an AI-policy super PAC in the Public First network. Crypto-sector federal donor balances were unchanged in the latest review.

Executive summary: America PAC disclosed two September 6 digital-media expenditures totaling $1,364,646 opposing Democrat James Talarico in Texas. Combined with MAGA Inc.'s September 5 filings, the two independent committees disclosed at least $16.36 million in Texas Senate activity over those two days: $11.36 million opposing Talarico and $5 million supporting Republican Ken Paxton. Separately, September 6 FEC-update tracking recorded $2.14 million across 24 Defending Our Values PAC filings targeting Briscoe Cain, Carlos De La Cruz Jr. and Alexandra Mealer. These committees are legally separate; this report does not infer coordination from parallel spending.

Campaign and issue interests

America PAC → Texas Senate

The September 6 filings move America PAC's Texas activity into higher-dollar digital media opposing Talarico. MAGA Inc. separately filed $5 million supporting Paxton and $5 million opposing Talarico on September 5. The parallel spending establishes concentration around the same race, but not coordination.

Defending Our Values → AI-policy House contests

The committee's stated mission is to support Republicans who favor AI policies centered on child safety, competition with China, national security and economic effects. FEC-derived tracking lists support for Carlos De La Cruz and Alexandra Mealer and opposition to Briscoe Cain among its candidate positions.

Crypto network

No new federal Fairshake-family donor receipt large enough to change the prior report's interpretation was identified. The existing network remains heavily capitalized and is tracked separately on the Crypto Donor Network page.

Observed outcomes

No new election result was recorded in the September 6–7 watch window for these newly reported expenditures. The Texas Senate and House general-election contests remain unresolved.

For AI spending, prior-cycle and primary outcomes remain mixed across different committees and candidates. This report records where money was deployed and later records actual election or policy outcomes; it does not attribute a win, loss or policy decision to spending without stronger evidence.

“Outcome” here means a certified or otherwise recorded election result, enacted policy, regulatory action, appointment, contract or other independently verifiable event—not polling movement or inferred persuasion.

Methodology and limitations

  • This update compares newly available filings and public reporting against the September 6 report. Only developments large enough to change the interpretation of donor deployment or network concentration are elevated as new.
  • FEC regular committee summaries can lag new activity and generally exclude the newest 24- and 48-hour independent-expenditure reports. Late-cycle transaction monitoring therefore relies on individual filings and reputable FEC-derived trackers as well as committee summary pages.
  • A daily sum of newly surfaced 24/48-hour filings may include amendments or corrected transactions. Such sums are reported as filing activity and are not automatically added to prior aggregate spending totals.
  • Independent expenditures support or oppose candidates without transferring funds to the candidate committee. Super PACs are prohibited from coordinating those expenditures with candidates.
  • Issue alignment is descriptive and based on committee missions, donor-network relationships, candidate positions and disclosed spending. It is not evidence of corruption, quid pro quo, contractual reward or regulatory favoritism.
  • No candidate, party, committee or policy is scored or ranked. Election and policy outcomes are recorded descriptively without assigning causation to campaign spending.
